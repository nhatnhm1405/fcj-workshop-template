I attended the Ho Chi Minh City watch party and followed the keynote streamed from Hanoi. The sessions discussed enterprise cloud and AI use, the foundations behind reliable services, security, and the need for validation as AI becomes part of software development.

### Key Highlights

#### Digital infrastructure and the cloud/AI ecosystem

The opening placed AI in the context of compute infrastructure, skilled people and collaboration across businesses, government, universities and research organizations. Cloud capabilities support digital transformation and technology development. The summary of the opening remarks was also checked against the [Ministry of Science and Technology's event report](https://mst.gov.vn/ai-viet-nam-tu-mo-rong-ha-tang-den-bai-toan-bien-cong-nghe-thanh-gia-tri-19726093007364899.htm).

#### Start with the customer problem

The cloud and AI examples illustrated why teams should define the problem, data and measurable outcome before choosing services. The situations discussed included healthcare information analysis, in-store product monitoring and agricultural operations.

#### AI in enterprise workflows

The Techcombank presentation described integrating AI into workflows rather than treating it as a standalone tool. Topics included connecting software requirements with development and testing, supporting incident handling, access governance, model selection and staff training.

#### Invisible engineering

Dr. Werner Vogels discussed engineering decisions behind services: understanding data access patterns and balancing performance, isolation and reliability. AWS Nitro System, Lambda and Firecracker illustrated infrastructure layers that end users do not normally see. The discussion of Amazon Time Sync Service and ClockBound extended the topic to time and uncertainty in distributed systems.

#### Validating AI-assisted software

The Katalon session highlighted the gap that can arise when changes are produced faster than they can be tested. Teams need to connect requirements, code changes, test cases, defects and operational data; evidence such as tests, logs and telemetry supports release decisions.

#### Validation debt and engineering accountability

Validation debt is verification work that accumulates when changes are produced faster than they can be assessed. Development speed should be paired with traceable testing, appropriate permissions and clear human responsibility.

### Lessons Learned

- Define the problem, intended users and outcomes before choosing a service or AI model.
- Include access governance and observability in the design.
- Use tests, logs and telemetry as evidence when evaluating software.
- Consider performance, cost, isolation and reliability together.
- Keep human accountability for risk assessment and release decisions when using AI tools.

### Applying the Learning

In the FCAJ Hackathon System project, these ideas can inform component permissions, observability and test evidence for the AI workflow. This is a proposed direction for application.
